ADU Financing

ADU Financing in Santa Clarita: HELOC vs. Cash-Out Refi vs. Construction Loan

By Harout — Sunny Home Loans·7 min read

If you own a home in Santa Clarita and you're thinking about building an ADU, the financing decision usually comes down to three options: a HELOC, a cash-out refinance, or a construction loan. Each one draws on your equity differently, and picking the wrong one can mean paying more than you need to — or running out of funds mid-build.

Why This Decision Matters More Than It Seems

All three options ultimately do the same thing — turn your home equity into cash for construction. But how and when you get that cash, what happens to your existing mortgage, and how the rate works can differ significantly. Getting this wrong doesn't just cost money; it can stall a build mid-project if you underestimate how funds are released.

Option 1: HELOC (Home Equity Line of Credit)

A HELOC gives you a revolving credit line against your home's equity. You draw funds as needed — pay the contractor for the foundation, draw more for framing, draw more for finishes — and you only pay interest on what you've actually used.

  • Best for: Homeowners who want flexibility and don't want to disturb their existing first mortgage
  • Rate type: Usually variable, tied to Prime
  • Typical requirement: Roughly 20% equity remaining after the draw
  • Key advantage: If your current mortgage rate is well below today's market rate, a HELOC leaves that first mortgage untouched

Option 2: Cash-Out Refinance

A cash-out refinance replaces your existing mortgage with a new, larger one, and you receive the difference in cash at closing. It's a single new loan rather than a second lien.

  • Best for: Homeowners who want the full amount in hand at once, or whose current rate isn't meaningfully better than today's rates
  • Rate type: Fixed or ARM, your choice
  • Typical requirement: Similar ~20% equity threshold, standard refinance underwriting
  • Key tradeoff: If your current rate is well below market, refinancing means giving that rate up on your entire mortgage balance, not just the new funds

Option 3: Construction Loan

A construction loan is a short-term loan sized against your property's projected value once the ADU is complete, not just its current equity. Funds are released in draws as construction milestones are met, then the loan typically converts to a permanent mortgage.

  • Best for: Larger builds, or homeowners with limited current equity but strong projected after-completion value
  • Rate type: Short-term rate during construction, converting to permanent financing after
  • Key advantage: Can unlock more funding than your current equity alone would support
  • Key tradeoff: More paperwork and a longer approval process than a HELOC or refi

Santa Clarita-Specific Considerations

Home values across Valencia, Canyon Country, Saugus, and Stevenson Ranch have held up well, which means many SCV homeowners have more accessible equity than they realize. Local ADU rents in the $1,500–$3,000/month range also mean an appraiser may be able to factor projected rental income into a cash-out refinance in some cases — worth asking about specifically, since it can change how much you qualify to borrow.

A Quick Way to Decide

  1. Check your current mortgage rate. If it's well below today's rates, lean HELOC to protect it.
  2. Check your equity position. If you're equity-rich, HELOC or cash-out refi both work. If equity is tight relative to the build cost, look at a construction loan.
  3. Check your timeline. Need funds fast and simple? Cash-out refi. Want to pay interest only as you draw? HELOC. Building something larger and more complex? Construction loan.

Is Financing an ADU Worth It?

With average ADU rents in Santa Clarita running $1,500 or more a month, most homeowners recoup their financing cost through rental income within a few years — while also adding meaningful resale value to the property. The financing structure you choose won't change whether it's worth doing; it changes how efficiently you get there.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a HELOC or cash-out refinance better for building an ADU?
A HELOC is usually better if you want to draw funds as construction phases complete and keep your existing low mortgage rate untouched. A cash-out refinance makes more sense if you want the full amount available at once and your current rate is already close to today's market rate.
How much equity do I need to finance an ADU in Santa Clarita?
Most lenders allow borrowing up to 80% of your home's value minus your existing mortgage balance. A home worth $750,000 with a $400,000 mortgage balance would have roughly $200,000 in accessible equity.
When does a construction loan make more sense than a HELOC?
Construction loans are typically the better fit for larger builds or when a homeowner has limited existing equity, since they can be sized against the property's projected future value rather than only its current equity.

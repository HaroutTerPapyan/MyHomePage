DSCR Loan vs. Conventional Investment Property Loan: Which Costs Less?

By Harout — Sunny Home Loans·6 min read

If you qualify for both a conventional investment property loan and a DSCR loan, which one actually costs less? The honest answer: it depends less on the rate sheet and more on your income type, your timeline, and whether you plan to scale your portfolio.

The Rate Difference

Conventional investment property loans typically carry the lowest available rate, since they're backed by Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac guidelines. DSCR loans usually run 0.5–1% higher, reflecting the more flexible qualification standard. On a single property, that difference is real but often modest in dollar terms.

Where DSCR Wins Beyond Rate

  • No personal income documentation — no tax returns, no W-2s, no employer verification
  • Faster closings — often 21–30 days versus 30–45+ for conventional
  • LLC vesting allowed — conventional loans require closing in your personal name
  • No cap on number of financed properties — conventional loans limit borrowers to a set number of financed properties; DSCR loans generally don't

Where Conventional Wins

  • Lower rate, which compounds meaningfully as your portfolio grows
  • Lower down payment options in some cases, particularly for a first investment property
  • No prepayment penalty in most cases, versus the step-down penalty common on DSCR loans

The Real Decision Factor: Scale

For an investor buying their second or third property, conventional loan limits on financed properties often become the deciding factor regardless of rate. Once you've hit that ceiling — or you're buying through an LLC for liability protection — DSCR becomes the only practical path forward, making the rate comparison somewhat academic.

A Simple Way to Decide

If this is your first or second investment property, you qualify easily on paper, and you don't need LLC vesting, run the numbers on both — the conventional rate savings may be worth the extra documentation. If you're scaling past a few properties, self-employed with complex income, or want liability protection through an LLC, DSCR is almost always the more practical choice regardless of the rate gap.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a DSCR loan more expensive than a conventional investment property loan?
Typically yes, by roughly 0.5–1% in rate. The tradeoff is faster closing, no personal income documentation, and the ability to vest in an LLC — advantages a conventional loan doesn't offer.
Can I use a conventional loan if I'm self-employed?
Yes, but it requires two years of tax returns and often results in a lower qualifying income than a DSCR loan would, since conventional underwriting is based on net income after business deductions.
Which loan closes faster?
DSCR loans generally close faster — often 21–30 days — since there's no employment verification or personal income documentation to collect and review.

CalHFA ADU Grant Explained: Up to $40K for Your Santa Clarita Build

By Harout — Sunny Home Loans·6 min read

One of the biggest hidden costs of building an ADU isn't the construction itself — it's everything that has to happen before a single shovel hits the ground. Architectural plans, permit fees, soil tests, and site prep can easily run $20,000–$40,000 before your builder even starts. California's CalHFA ADU Grant program exists specifically to offset those costs.

What the Grant Actually Covers

The CalHFA ADU Grant provides funding — up to $40,000 for eligible homeowners — toward predevelopment expenses. This is a meaningful distinction from construction financing: the grant is designed to remove the upfront barrier that stops many homeowners from even getting to the point of breaking ground.

  • Architectural and engineering plans
  • Permit and application fees
  • Soil tests and impact fees
  • Site preparation work required before construction begins

Construction costs themselves — the actual building of the ADU — are financed separately, typically through a HELOC, cash-out refinance, or construction loan.

Does It Need to Be Repaid?

No, as long as the ADU gets built. This functions as a true grant rather than a loan, which makes it fundamentally different from the financing options used for the construction phase.

Who Typically Qualifies

  • Income limits apply, based on area median income for the county — these limits vary by household size, so eligibility isn't a flat cutoff
  • The property must be the homeowner's primary residence in most program versions
  • The ADU must meet local zoning and building requirements for the jurisdiction, which in Santa Clarita means complying with the city's specific ADU ordinance

How to Combine the Grant With Your Construction Financing

The most efficient sequence for most Santa Clarita homeowners looks like this:

  1. Confirm CalHFA ADU Grant eligibility and apply for predevelopment funding first
  2. Use the grant to cover plans, permits, and site prep without touching your home equity
  3. Line up construction financing — a HELOC, cash-out refinance, or construction loan — once you have approved plans and a permit in hand
  4. Draw construction funds as the build progresses, keeping your out-of-pocket cash outlay to a minimum throughout

This sequencing means the grant absorbs the riskiest, most uncertain phase of the project — before you know your final permitted plans — while your equity-based financing is reserved for the phase you can budget with much more precision.

What This Means for Santa Clarita Homeowners

With SCV rents for a well-built ADU regularly landing in the $1,500–$3,000/month range, reducing your upfront predevelopment cost by up to $40,000 meaningfully shortens the time it takes for the ADU to pay for itself. It's one of the few programs that directly targets the exact phase of ADU building that stalls the most projects before they start.

Next Steps

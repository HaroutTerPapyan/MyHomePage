Can I Close a DSCR Loan in My LLC? What LA Investors Should Know

By Harout — Sunny Home Loans·6 min read

If you're building a rental portfolio in Los Angeles, chances are your attorney or CPA has already told you to hold property in an LLC. The good news: DSCR loans are one of the few mortgage products built to accommodate exactly that. Here's what actually happens when you close in an entity's name.

Why Investors Want LLC Vesting in the First Place

Holding rental property in an LLC separates your personal assets from liability tied to the property — if a tenant sues over an injury on the property, the claim generally stops at the LLC's assets rather than reaching your personal bank accounts or other real estate. For investors scaling past one or two properties, this separation becomes increasingly important, and many hold each property in its own single-member LLC for maximum protection.

Why This Is Hard With Conventional Loans

Conventional and government-backed loans (Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, FHA) require the loan to close in your personal name — full stop. This is one of the primary reasons investors move to DSCR and other Non-QM products once they're serious about scaling: it's often the only path to entity-level ownership with standard mortgage financing.

How LLC Vesting Actually Works With a DSCR Loan

  1. The LLC must typically be formed before application — most lenders want to see the entity already exists, with an EIN and operating agreement in place, rather than being created mid-transaction.
  2. A personal guarantee is almost always required. The LLC holds title, but you personally guarantee repayment. This is the part that surprises new investors — the LLC provides liability protection for the property, not a shield from the loan obligation itself.
  3. Multi-member LLCs need extra documentation. If you're investing with partners, expect to provide the operating agreement showing ownership percentages, and possibly personal guarantees from each member with meaningful ownership stake.
  4. Single-purpose entities are common and generally fine. Many investors form a new LLC for each property. Lenders are used to this and it doesn't typically create friction, as long as the entity is properly documented.

What LA Investors Should Set Up Before Applying

  • LLC formed with the California Secretary of State, in good standing
  • Federal EIN obtained for the entity
  • Operating agreement drafted, especially critical for multi-member LLCs
  • A business bank account under the LLC's name, ideally with some history before application

None of this needs to happen the week before you apply — investors who plan to scale in LA's competitive market often set up their LLC structure well ahead of finding a property, so financing never becomes the bottleneck once an offer is accepted.

Common Pitfalls With LLC-Vested DSCR Loans

  • Forming the LLC too late. A brand-new entity with no history can sometimes raise additional underwriting questions — earlier formation is smoother.
  • Assuming all members are treated equally. Lenders often only require guarantees from members owning 20-25% or more — worth clarifying with your specific lender's guidelines before assuming everyone needs to sign.
  • Not confirming insurance requirements. Properties held in an LLC typically need a commercial or landlord policy naming the entity, not a standard homeowner's policy.

The Bottom Line for LA Investors

If asset protection is part of your investment strategy — and for most investors building a portfolio in a litigious market like Los Angeles, it should be — a DSCR loan is likely your most straightforward path to financing property directly in an LLC's name. The key is having your entity properly formed and documented before you're under contract, so it never slows down your closing timeline.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I close a DSCR loan under a multi-member LLC?
Yes in most cases, though guidelines vary by lender — some require all members to personally guarantee the loan, while others only require the managing member. Confirming this early avoids delays once the file is already in underwriting.
Do I need a personal guarantee if I close in an LLC?
Almost always, yes. DSCR lenders generally require a personal guarantee from the LLC's members even though the loan closes in the entity's name. The LLC provides liability protection for the property itself, not a way to avoid personal responsibility for the loan.
Can I transfer a property I already own into an LLC after closing?
It's possible, but it can trigger a due-on-sale clause with your existing lender and may have tax implications. It's generally simpler to close the purchase directly in the LLC's name from the start.

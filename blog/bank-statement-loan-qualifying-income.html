How Bank Statement Loans Calculate Your Qualifying Income

By Harout — Sunny Home Loans·6 min read

The number that determines what you qualify for on a bank statement loan isn't your gross deposits — it's a calculated figure that runs your deposits through an expense assumption first. Understanding this calculation before you apply means no surprises when your lender comes back with a number.

The Two Pieces of the Calculation

Every bank statement income calculation comes down to two inputs: your average monthly deposits, and the expense ratio applied against them.

Qualifying Income = Average Monthly Deposits × (1 − Expense Ratio)

Step 1: Averaging Your Deposits

The lender totals your qualifying deposits across your statement period — 12 or 24 months — and divides by the number of months. Large, clearly-identifiable transfers between your own accounts are typically excluded, as are one-time items like loan proceeds or gifts, since those aren't recurring income.

Step 2: Applying the Expense Ratio

This is where personal and business accounts diverge:

  • Personal bank statements: If your income is deposited directly into a personal account with no business overhead running through it, the full deposit amount is often usable without any reduction.
  • Business bank statements: A standard expense ratio — commonly 50% for sole proprietors — is applied, since a portion of what flows through a business account covers overhead like payroll, supplies, or rent rather than personal income.
  • CPA-prepared P&L override: If your actual expense ratio is lower than the standard assumption, a CPA-prepared profit and loss statement can sometimes be used instead, improving your qualifying number.

A Worked Example

Say a self-employed contractor deposits an average of $18,000/month into their business account over 24 months. At a standard 50% expense ratio, the qualifying income comes to $9,000/month — roughly $108,000/year. If a CPA-prepared P&L shows actual overhead closer to 30%, the qualifying income could rise to $12,600/month instead, a meaningful difference in purchasing power.

Why This Matters Before You Shop for a Home

Knowing your likely qualifying income before you start house hunting in Los Angeles' competitive market saves time and prevents disappointment. It also tells you whether pulling together a CPA-prepared P&L is worth the effort — for some borrowers, it can meaningfully change the price range they're shopping in.

Getting an Accurate Estimate

The fastest way to know your real number is to send a lender your recent statements directly rather than estimating on your own — averaging deposits correctly (excluding transfers, one-time items, and other exclusions) is easy to get wrong without seeing the actual calculation applied.

Frequently Asked Questions

What expense ratio do lenders typically use for a sole proprietor?
A 50% expense factor is the common default, meaning half of your gross deposits are treated as business overhead and half as qualifying income, unless a CPA-prepared profit and loss statement supports a different figure.
Can a P&L statement improve my qualifying income?
Yes. A CPA-prepared profit and loss statement showing your actual expense ratio can sometimes replace the standard 50% default, which helps borrowers whose real overhead is lower than the assumed rate.
Do lenders average 12 or 24 months of deposits?
Whichever statement period you provide. Twenty-four months tends to produce a more stable average and often better pricing, while 12 months is the minimum most lenders will accept.

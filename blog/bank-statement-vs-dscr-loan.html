Bank Statement vs. DSCR Loan: Which Fits Your Situation?

By Harout — Sunny Home Loans·7 min read

Bank statement loans and DSCR loans get grouped together often — both skip traditional tax return documentation, both fall under the Non-QM umbrella, and both are popular with self-employed borrowers and investors in Los Angeles. But they qualify you in fundamentally different ways, and picking the wrong one can mean unnecessary paperwork or a worse rate.

The Core Difference

A bank statement loan qualifies you — using your personal or business deposits as a stand-in for tax return income. A DSCR loan qualifies the property — using its rental income relative to its mortgage payment, without looking at your personal income at all.

When a Bank Statement Loan Is the Right Choice

  • You're self-employed and buying a primary residence — DSCR isn't an option here, since it's investment-property only
  • Your business deposits meaningfully exceed what your tax returns show after write-offs
  • You don't need or want to hold the property in an LLC

When a DSCR Loan Is the Right Choice

  • You're buying or refinancing an investment property, not a home to live in
  • You want to close in an LLC for liability protection
  • You'd rather qualify based on the property's cash flow than dig through 12–24 months of personal or business statements
  • You're a W-2 employee investing in real estate on the side — DSCR doesn't require self-employment at all

A Common LA Scenario: Using Both

It's common for a self-employed Los Angeles investor to use a bank statement loan for their own primary residence, then use DSCR loans for every investment property they add afterward. The two products aren't competing options — they're often complementary tools used for different purposes within the same portfolio.

What Both Products Have in Common

  • Both fall under Non-QM lending, meaning more flexible guidelines than conventional or government-backed loans
  • Both typically carry a modest rate premium over conventional financing
  • Both skip the tax-return documentation that trips up self-employed and investor borrowers on conventional loans
  • Both are widely available in the California market, including throughout the LA and Santa Clarita area

How to Decide

Start with one question: is this property something you'll live in, or something you're renting out? A primary residence points you toward a bank statement loan (if you're self-employed) or conventional financing (if you're not). An investment property points you toward a DSCR loan almost every time, regardless of your income type.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use a DSCR loan to buy my primary residence?
No. DSCR loans are for investment properties only. A bank statement loan is the option to use if you're self-employed and buying a primary residence.
Which loan is better for a self-employed investor buying a rental?
A DSCR loan is usually the better fit, since it skips personal income documentation entirely and qualifies based on the property's rental income instead.
Can I combine both loan types across different properties?
Yes. Many investors use a bank statement loan for their primary residence and DSCR loans for each investment property, since the two products qualify borrowers differently and aren't mutually exclusive.
